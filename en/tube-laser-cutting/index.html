WHAT IT IS

What is tube and profile laser cutting?

Tube and profile laser cutting is laser cutting applied to long sections instead of flat sheet. A cutting program is generated from the part drawing, and the cut to length, holes, slots and end shapes are made on the different faces of the section in one sequence, without re-clamping the part. What comes off is a part ready for welding or assembly, not a length of stock that still needs sawing, drilling and grinding.

These sections go by many names. Box section — square or rectangular hollow tube — is itself a kind of profile. Alongside it, round pipe, open profiles such as angle, channel and U sections, and oval and special sections can all be laser cut. HyperFab does this work in mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium.

HyperFab’s team has long experience in profile laser cutting and focuses on what shows up at assembly: accurate lengths and hole positions, clean cut edges and parts that match across a batch. Orders are not limited to production runs either: samples and small first runs are accepted, so the part can be tried in a real assembly before the batch is cut.

Your material or ours: you can supply the tube, pipe or profile yourself, or have HyperFab source it. Just say which when you ask for a quote.
WHAT CAN BE CUT

Which features can be cut on tube and profiles?

Cut to length, round and shaped holes, slots and windows, coped pipe ends, mitred ends and tab-and-slot features for welded frames; what is possible depends on the section and material.

Cut to length

Lengths cut accurately with clean ends, so every part in a batch comes out the same — no sawing or marking out.

Round and shaped holes

Round, oval, square or any other hole shape on the faces of the section, positioned accurately from the part end. Very small holes relative to the wall thickness are confirmed after review.

Slots and windows

Slots for fasteners and adjustment, windows for cable routing or weight saving, and decorative cut-outs in tube and pipe walls.

Coped pipe ends

Pipe ends shaped to fit the mating pipe (fish-mouth or saddle cuts), so the two sit without gaps and weld evenly. On thick-wall pipe, after reviewing the drawing.

Mitred ends

Angled end cuts for frame and box corners; the achievable angle depends on the section.

Tab-and-slot frames

Tabs and slots that locate frame members in the right place before welding, reducing fixturing and improving frame accuracy.

On request, after review: weld-prep bevels and laser marking or etching can be considered on request, depending on the section. On open profiles and special sections, the range of features is confirmed once the drawing has been reviewed.
WHY LASER

Why laser cut instead of sawing, drilling and punching?

Every cut and hole in one setup and one program, with no tooling cost, holes positioned accurately from the part end and matching parts across the batch.

DESIGN TIPS

How should a tube or profile part be drawn?

A 3D STEP or IGES file is the best input; state the section, material and quantity, keep holes off the corner radii and allow for the weld seam of welded tube.

3D file: a STEP or IGES model of the part is the best input. If you do not have one, a 2D drawing, DXF or PDF is accepted, as long as the section and dimensions are clearly stated.

Section, material and quantity: note the section size and wall thickness, the material (mild steel, stainless steel or aluminium) and the quantity of each part.

Clear of corner radii: box section has rounded corners, and a hole that runs onto the radius changes shape. Keep the edges of holes and slots on the flat part of the face.

Tab-and-slot clearance: leave a little clearance in tabs and slots so parts fit together without forcing.

Weld seam: welded tube and pipe have a weld seam on the inside. If a hole or slot must not land on the seam, say so on the drawing.

Visible faces: if a face will be visible in the finished product, mark it so feature placement and handling take it into account.

Threads in thin sheet and walls: thin sheet or tube wall cannot hold a strong tapped thread, so holes are cut for rivet nuts instead, which give screws a proper thread (St37 mechanism frame case study).

ONE CHAIN

Can HyperFab also weld and finish the frame?

Yes. A tube or profile part is usually one member of a frame, chassis or structure. After laser cutting, you can also hand HyperFab the TIG and MIG welding and frame fabrication, followed by powder coating or plating and anodizing. Sheet parts for the same structure, such as base plates, brackets and gussets, can be ordered online through sheet metal laser cutting and sheet metal bending on hyperfab.ir.

The advantage of one chain is one responsible party. If tabs and slots were designed for welding, HyperFab, having been responsible for the cut, is also responsible for welding the frame and checking the final dimensions. The part is not passed between workshops, and if something is wrong, it is clear who fixes it.

Frequently asked questions

Which sections and materials can be laser cut?
Square and rectangular tube (box section), round pipe, open profiles such as angle, channel and U, and oval and special sections, in mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium.
Do you accept samples or small quantities?
Yes. HyperFab accepts samples and small first runs so the part can be tried in a real assembly before batch production. Once approved, the same program is used for the batch order.
Can I supply my own tube or profile?
Yes. You can supply the material yourself or have HyperFab source it. Say which you prefer when you ask for a quote.
What file do you need for tube laser cutting?
A 3D STEP or IGES file is the best input. A 2D drawing, DXF or PDF is also accepted, as long as the section, material and dimensions are clear. If you only have a physical sample part, you can bring that too.
What are the maximum size, wall thickness and accuracy?
They depend on the section and material. What is achievable for each project is confirmed by phone after the drawing has been reviewed: +98 996 223 3898.
Can you also weld and paint the frame?
Yes. After laser cutting, TIG and MIG welding and frame fabrication, powder coating, and plating or anodizing are also available at HyperFab, so the structure arrives finished.
How is tube laser cutting different from sheet laser cutting?
Sheet laser cutting works on flat sheet and is ordered online at hyperfab.ir with an instant price. Tube and profile laser cutting works on tube, pipe and profiles, covers several faces and end shapes, and is ordered by phone after the drawing has been reviewed.
