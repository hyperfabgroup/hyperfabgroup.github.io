Custom Aluminium Extrusion: Die, Press Tonnage and Section Design

Updated: 15 November 2026

To order a custom aluminium profile, the section is finalised in consultation with HyperFab’s engineering team, a dedicated extrusion die is built, and the profile is produced at the ordered tonnage. You bring the required tonnage and pay for the die. Die type, section size relative to press capacity and sound section design decide feasibility and cost.

Short answer: how do you order a custom aluminium profile?

HyperFab specialises in aluminium extrusion, and aluminium only: other metals such as steel, brass and copper are not part of this service. The model is simple. You bring the required tonnage and pay for the die; section consultation, die making, profile production, quality control and delivery are handled by HyperFab’s engineering team.

This is a batch service, ordered by tonnage, meaning the weight of profile you order. The die cost and the profile price, which depends on alloy, weight and tonnage, are quoted separately. You will see both “aluminium” and “aluminum” in specifications; it is the same material and the same process.

What is aluminium extrusion and how does it work?

Aluminium extrusion pushes a hot aluminium billet under press force through a steel die to make a long profile with a constant cross-section. The heated billet is loaded into the press container, the ram drives it towards the die, and the metal emerges through the die opening in the shape of the section. The profile is then cooled, straightened and cut to length, and where needed aged to reach its final temper.

Almost any section you can draw can be made with a dedicated die: solid, hollow, slotted, finned or with screw ports, which is why extrusion is the most economical way to make long, uniform aluminium parts. But extrusion dies are expensive, and a poorly designed section can lead to warped profiles, uneven surfaces or a broken die. That is why the section is reviewed before the die is made.

Solid, semi-hollow and hollow extrusion dies: what is the difference?

The shape of the section decides the die type, not the buyer’s preference. The more fully enclosed voids a section has, the more complex and expensive the die becomes.

An extrusion die is designed and built for your section and produces only your profile, so it is part of your product’s tooling cost and the customer pays for it. It is quoted separately from the profile price. Agree how the die is stored and used for repeat orders with HyperFab when you receive the quotation.

Press tonnage and the circumscribing circle: what limits section size?

In extrusion, “tonnage” can mean two things. Press tonnage is the force and capacity of the press that extrudes the profile; order tonnage is the weight of profile you buy. When HyperFab talks about the “required tonnage”, it means order tonnage. Whether your section suits the available press capacity is checked during section consultation.

The circumscribing circle is the smallest circle that fully encloses the cross-section. Each press, with its container and dies, can only extrude sections whose circle falls within its range, so a larger section needs a larger press.

Size is not the only limit. Walls that are very thin for the size of the section, several enclosed voids and harder-to-extrude alloys all need more pressure, so a section can fit the circle and still be difficult to extrude. If a section sits near the edge of the production range, splitting it into two profiles that snap or screw together is sometimes the more practical answer; that option is explored in consultation.

Extrusion section design rules

These rules extend die life and lower the cost per kilo of profile. HyperFab’s engineering team checks all of them before the die is built and suggests changes where needed:

  • Uniform wall thickness: large differences unbalance metal flow and warp the profile.
  • Rounded corners: give sharp corners a small radius for longer die life and a better surface.
  • Balanced section: symmetric, balanced sections extrude more stably and accurately.
  • Avoid deep, narrow channels: they weaken the die tongue, so the depth-to-width ratio must be controlled.
  • Enclose voids only where needed: if the function allows, opening one void can move the die from hollow to a simpler type.
  • Tight tolerances on functional dimensions only; extruded profile tolerances are typically specified per EN 755.

Which aluminium alloy and temper for extrusion? (6000 series)

Most extruded profiles are made from 6000-series alloys, with 6063 and 6061 the most widely used. 6063 extrudes more easily and gives a better surface, which suits architectural and decorative profiles, thin and intricate sections, and parts that will be anodized. 6061 is stronger and is chosen for structural work, but it is harder to extrude, and thin, complex sections are more demanding in it.

The temper is part of the profile specification too and is usually written on the drawing with a designation such as T5 or T6. If you know the alloy and temper, put them on the drawing; if not, describe the use, the loads and the final finish and the engineering team will advise. The alloy also affects the profile price.

An extruded profile is rarely the finished product. Anodizing, powder coating, machining, punching and aluminium welding are also available at HyperFab.

How to order a custom aluminium profile, and what drives the price

Ordering takes five steps: send the section drawing, a DXF or even a sketch with the profile’s use; section consultation and tonnage advice; a quotation with die cost and profile price shown separately; die making and a sample profile for your approval; and finally production at the ordered tonnage, inspection and delivery. The price comes from the factors below, and no meaningful figure can be given before the section has been reviewed.

  • Die type and complexity: solid, semi-hollow or hollow.
  • Section size and its circumscribing circle relative to the production range.
  • Section weight and wall thickness.
  • Alloy and temper.
  • Order tonnage.
  • Tolerances and surface requirements.
  • Follow-on work such as anodizing, powder coating and machining.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the extrusion die?
The customer pays for the dedicated die, and it is built only for that customer’s section. Agree ownership, storage and use of the die for later orders with HyperFab when you receive the quotation.
Is there a minimum order for aluminium extrusion?
HyperFab takes extrusion orders by tonnage. The right tonnage depends on the size and weight of the section and is advised after consultation.
Does HyperFab extrude metals other than aluminium?
No. In extrusion, HyperFab works with aluminium only. If you need brass or steel parts, HyperFab’s batch CNC turning may suit, depending on the part.
What drawing or file do you need?
A section drawing, a DXF or even a hand sketch, together with the profile’s use, is enough to start. Add the alloy, temper and final finish if you know them; you can also send the details by phone or on Bale.
