Mechanism Frame in St37: From Design to Welding

Published: 16 November 2026

HyperFab took a light, low-cost mechanism frame from design to final weld in house: St37 steel box section was cut on a tube laser, the sheet parts were laser cut and bent, everything was joined by TIG welding, and rivet nuts were fitted so parts could be screwed to the thin sheet. The videos on this page were filmed on this job in HyperFab’s workshop.

The brief: a light, low-cost frame for a mechanism

This frame was designed and made for a HyperFab customer’s mechanism: a structure the mechanism’s parts are mounted on. The aim of the design was a frame that is strong enough for the job but light and inexpensive.

On a job like this, the choice of section, sheet thickness and joining method sets the final cost from day one. So HyperFab started with the design, not with the cutting.

Designed by HyperFab

HyperFab designed the frame around two elements: St37 steel box section for the skeleton, and St37 sheet, laser cut and bent into the frame’s sheet parts. With laser cutting, holes and joint positions can be settled in the design from the start, so less drilling and hand fitting is needed after fabrication.

One key design decision was to use thin sheet. Thinner sheet cuts both the weight of the frame and the cost of material and cutting, but it is too thin to hold a usable thread. Rivet nuts solved that, as explained below.

Tube laser cutting the St37 box section

The skeleton parts were cut from St37 box section on a tube laser. Tube laser cutting produces the length, the holes and the end profile that mates with the neighbouring part in a single operation, straight from the design file. The result is parts that fit together accurately, so the welder does not have to grind and file joints to make them meet.

Sheet laser cutting and bending in HyperFab’s workshops

The frame’s sheet parts were laser cut from St37 sheet in HyperFab’s workshops and then bent. Laser cutting produces the flat pattern with every hole and slot in one go, and bending brings it to its final shape.

Bent flanges stiffen thin sheet, which is why a thinner gauge can still give a plate that does not flex under load. This is where design and fabrication meet: thin sheet only works when the bends are designed for it.

TIG welding the St37 frame

Next, the laser-cut box-section parts were welded to the cut and bent sheet. This was done by TIG welding, whose heat is concentrated and easy to control: it is less likely to burn through or distort thin sheet, and it leaves a clean weld that needs little grinding.

Laser-cut parts fit together properly, which makes assembly and welding simpler and the drawing dimensions easier to hold.

Rivet nuts in thin sheet: strong threads without thick sheet

Thin sheet kept the frame light and affordable, but it is too thin to hold a reliable thread: a thread with only a turn or two of engagement strips after the screw has been in and out a few times. The answer was rivet nuts.

A rivet nut goes into a hole from one side of the sheet, and the setting tool collapses its body behind the sheet so it locks firmly in place. On this job the rivet nuts were set with a pneumatic rivet-nut tool, which makes the work fast and consistent. The benefits:

  • A full, strong thread for screws without moving to thicker sheet
  • Fitted from one side, with no access needed behind the part
  • No welding and no heat on the thin sheet, so no distortion
  • Less weight and cost than thicker sheet

The result: one team from design to finished frame

The result was a light frame in St37 box section and sheet, with the mechanism’s parts screwed to it. The more important point is how it was made: the design, tube laser cutting, sheet laser cutting and bending, welding and rivet nuts were all done by HyperFab, with one team responsible for the whole job.

When the designer and the fabricator are the same team, decisions such as thinning the sheet or choosing rivet nuts are made with the workshop in mind, and the final cost comes down. If your machine or mechanism needs a frame or a steel structure, send HyperFab a drawing or even an early idea.
